# HighLevel Agency First Month Operating Plan

The first month tests whether a specific agency buyer will pay for a clearly bounded implementation and whether you can deliver it economically. Use one offer and one primary market. Build enough proof to sell, then give prospecting and conversations consistent time.

Use two acquisition channels: researched UK agency outreach and matched Upwork applications. Plan five to ten marketplace applications per week only where suitable jobs exist. Track their cost and preparation time. Community participation and certification can support credibility later; they are not additional daily quotas.

## Weekly plan

| Week | Work | Output |
|---|---|---|
| One | Build the demonstration, record the video, prepare the one-page offer and research 25 UK agencies | One working demo, offer and 25 scored prospects; first 15 introductions |
| Two | Add 20 qualified agencies, send relevant introductions, follow up with week-one contacts and take discovery calls | 45 researched prospects in total; about 35 first contacts in total; recorded objections |
| Three | Add the final 15 agencies, finish 60 first contacts, present scoped proposals and begin a paid pilot if sold | 60 qualified first contacts in total; project scope and payment milestone where a buyer is ready |
| Four | Complete the pilot, ask about the next project, continue permitted follow-ups and review the funnel | First accepted project if sold; evidence, actual hours and a channel decision |

Activity counts are targets, not promised replies or sales. Use discovery quality and paid decisions to evaluate progress. If delivery starts earlier, protect the commitment and reduce outreach volume temporarily.

## Default weekly time allocation

Start with a 25-hour workweek for this experiment and adjust around actual projects:

- Six hours for prospect research and individual outreach.
- Two hours for matched marketplace applications and reviewing their cost and fit.
- Four hours for follow-ups, discovery calls and proposals.
- Eight hours for the demo, paid delivery or portfolio improvement.
- Three hours for documentation, testing improvements and reusable assets.
- Two hours for finances, scorecard review and planning.

A paid sprint may need up to 12 delivery hours. In that week, move hours from portfolio improvement and research, and schedule fewer new calls. Keep at least a small prospecting block so one delivery project does not empty the pipeline.

Do not use flexible hours to imply unlimited availability. Publish an overlap window you can maintain and leave room for interruptions.

Protect a sixty-to-ninety-minute acquisition block on working days within the allocations above. Urgent client incidents and committed delivery come first; reschedule the block instead of letting prospecting disappear. Do not promise overnight completion merely because time zones overlap.

## First ten working days

| Day | Main action | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| One | Select the fictional client and draw its lead journey | Source, owner, follow-up, booking and stop rules are explicit |
| Two | Build source, pipeline and calendar | Controlled enquiry appears in the correct place |
| Three | Build and test three workflows | Reply, opt-out, booking and reschedule behaviours pass |
| Four | Record the demo and create the proposal | Video is under four minutes and shows behaviour rather than menus |
| Five | Research the first 15 agencies and send five introductions | Every introduction has a truthful, specific reason |
| Six | Research ten more agencies and send five introductions | List reaches 25; messages and next actions recorded |
| Seven | Send five introductions and prepare for discovery | First-week total reaches 15 introductions |
| Eight | Follow up where due, add qualified prospects and take calls | Each reply has a next action or a closed status |
| Nine | Send relevant proposals or matched marketplace applications | Scope and proof address the buyer's actual requirement |
| Ten | Review the funnel and adjust the next week's messages | One specific bottleneck and one adjustment are recorded |

If a buyer appears sooner, prioritise the paid opportunity. Do not insist on finishing a perfect portfolio first.

## Weekly business scorecard

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified prospects researched | Unique agencies meeting the documented criteria | Separates useful targeting from large contact lists |
| First contacts | Unique prospects receiving a first relevant message | Measures acquisition effort without counting follow-ups twice |
| Positive replies | Responses showing interest or a relevant delivery need | Indicates whether targeting and the message connect |
| Held discovery calls | Conversations actually completed | Avoids treating a booking as a sales conversation |
| Qualified opportunities | Confirmed project, authority, access, budget and timing | Identifies work worth proposing |
| Paid pilots | Projects with agreed scope and first payment received | Tests willingness to pay |
| Accepted projects | Projects passing agreed tests and accepted by the buyer | Tests delivery quality |
| Repeat buyers | Buyers purchasing another project or renewing support | Tests the recurring business hypothesis |
| Delivery hours | All build, test, revision, call and handover time | Reveals whether the price supports the work |
| Contribution | Service revenue minus attributed delivery labour and direct costs | Measures economics before broader overhead and tax |
| Acquisition time per buyer | Research, outreach, call and proposal hours divided by new buyers | Exposes expensive sales processes |
| Monthly recurring revenue | Contracted recurring service fees for the month | Keeps one-time projects separate from retained revenue |

Calculate reply rate as positive replies divided by first contacts. Calculate proposal win rate as accepted paid projects divided by proposals with a decision. For small samples, use the underlying counts alongside the percentages; one win from two proposals is not a stable benchmark.

## Spending rules

Spend first on a functional demo environment, professional business identity and the tools required for the chosen deliverable. Use a trial only if you can complete the demonstration and evaluate the product within its terms. A Starter subscription at US$97/month is sufficient for a small demo setup when its capacity fits. [HighLevel pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing).

Do not buy lead databases, several outbound subscriptions, advanced AI packages or annual platform plans before the first sales conversations establish a need. Work in partner-owned accounts so each agency does not create an additional platform subscription expense for you.

Before accepting an overseas payment, agree the invoice currency, amount, payment route, responsibility for fees and refund terms. Use a payment provider available to your business and keep invoices and receipts. Establish the appropriate accounting and tax treatment with your accountant before trading; this kit does not calculate tax liabilities.

First confirm GST registration requirements and whether the engagement qualifies as an export of services. The GST portal describes LUT filing before the qualifying supply for registered persons choosing the eligible route to export without payment of integrated tax; the LUT relates to the financial year. It is not a blanket step for every foreign invoice. Confirm the required remittance records for your actual payment route with your accountant and bank. [Official GST LUT guidance](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/refund/Furnishing_of_Letter_of_Undertaking_for_Export_of_Goods_or_Services.htm).

## End of month review

Write one page answering:

1. Which buyer had the clearest and most immediate need?
2. Which opening message produced relevant conversations?
3. What prevented people from buying?
4. Did anyone pay the proposed price?
5. How many hours did the project actually take?
6. What would the same work cost to delegate responsibly?
7. Did the first buyer ask for another project or support?
8. Which single change should improve the next month?

Continue the offer if paid demand and delivery contribution are encouraging. Refine the message if relevant buyers engage but do not understand the service. Refine scope or pricing if projects sell but consume too much time. Test Indian agency partners if the overseas channel has received a proper targeting and follow-up test without enough useful conversation.

Direct Indian business expansion requires two accepted paid projects, one repeat purchase or renewal, positive actual delivery contribution, and six hours a week available for a two-week discovery exercise. Review this condition at the end of the month; do not force expansion on day 45. Keep profitable export work unless a comparison of contribution, acquisition effort, payments and retention supports reallocating capacity.

## What to postpone

Keep full websites, advertising management, SEO, social content, AI voice agents, large migrations, complex integrations and software resale outside the first offer. Add one only when a buyer has a specific need, you can deliver it, and the quote supports its true cost.

The first month succeeds when it produces a useful commercial decision: a repeatable paid offer, or clear evidence about which buyer, scope or channel needs changing. Revenue remains the priority; activity and learning should move you toward a paid engagement.
