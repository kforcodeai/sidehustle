# HighLevel Delivery and Repeat Business

Repeat work comes from a continuing need and a dependable delivery experience. You cannot ensure that every client returns. You can make a second purchase easier by delivering what was agreed, keeping the system understandable and proposing the next useful piece of work before the engagement ends.

## Before accepting a project

Confirm the buyer's current problem, the included client account, intended customer journey, scope, approver, price, payment milestones and acceptance tests. Identify whether existing automations touch the same contacts so the new work does not create competing messages or opportunities.

Use named, least-privilege access rather than shared credentials. The partner controls its own agency account; the end client should retain control of its domains, advertising assets and business messaging identity. Use synthetic contacts for development wherever possible. Limit customer data access to the work required.

Record who approves copy, consent rules, sending schedules, customer handoff and production activation. Do not activate production messaging without explicit buyer approval.

## Seven business day delivery sequence

| Stage | Work | Evidence supplied |
|---|---|---|
| Before day one | Complete deposit, account access, dependencies and scope | Agreed checklist and delivery start date |
| Day one | Map existing behaviour and the intended journey; record a baseline | Process map, known dependencies and acceptance cases |
| Days two and three | Configure source, pipeline, calendar and three draft workflows | Build progress and any decisions needed |
| Days four and five | Test happy paths and exceptions using controlled contacts | Completed functional test record |
| Day six | Demonstrate the build; collect one consolidated feedback list | Walkthrough and revision list |
| Day seven when approvals are complete | Apply scoped changes, obtain acceptance, collect final milestone and activate with approval | Final record, handover and support options |

The schedule moves if buyer feedback, channel approvals or access arrive late. Tell the buyer the cause and revised date before the original deadline. If the technical task changes materially, agree the change before building it.

## Acceptance checklist

Test these behaviours against the agreed process. Record the test contact, timestamp, expected result, observed result and pass or fail. Use controlled delivery to designated test recipients; do not enrol real customer lists for a demonstration.

| Case | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|
| New enquiry | Correct contact and opportunity created or updated; required source recorded |
| Repeated form submission | No unintended duplicate opportunity or repeated message sequence |
| Owner assignment | Intended owner receives the notification; the agreed fallback is visible |
| Missing required contact field | Record is flagged or routed safely rather than silently discarded |
| Valid eligible follow-up | Approved message is sent through the agreed channel at the agreed time |
| Recipient replies | Automated sales follow-up stops or hands over as designed |
| Recipient books | Sales follow-up stops and booking actions begin |
| Opt-out or channel restriction | Further messages on the restricted channel are suppressed |
| Appointment rescheduled | Old reminders do not fire for the previous time; new reminders follow the new time |
| Appointment cancelled | Pending appointment reminders stop; only approved next steps occur |
| Time zone or business hours | Scheduling follows the configured client time zone and sending window |
| Opportunity closed | Contact does not remain in an inappropriate sales chase |
| Failed action | Failure is visible in the available logs or agreed review process; do not imply universal automatic error detection |
| Existing workflow overlap | New and existing processes do not trigger conflicting actions |
| Handover | Buyer can identify the owner, change the approved copy and find the workflow map |

A test passes when the configured behaviour meets scope, not merely when a message is generated. Record provider-dependent limitations and open defects before acceptance.

## Messaging and India constraints

For WhatsApp, obtain and preserve the relevant opt-in, use approved templates when required and maintain a clear human handoff. Honour channel restrictions and opt-outs throughout the journey. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy).

Do not sell an India phone package using assumptions copied from US tutorials. HighLevel's India phone documentation currently describes local-number beta availability, domestic outbound restrictions, and unsupported features including SMS, MMS and Voice AI for those India numbers. Confirm the actual account, provider and feature before quoting any phone-dependent work. [HighLevel India phone documentation](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000008083-india-phone-numbers-voice-calling).

Keep the initial sprint on an already eligible channel. Phone setup, AI agents and new integrations become separate work after availability and cost are confirmed.

## Handover package

Provide the agreed workflow map, the named source and calendar, tags and fields used, message copy, test record, known dependencies and change history. Include a short recording showing how to pause the workflow and where to inspect an issue.

If a reusable snapshot is appropriate, remove client-specific secrets and data and check what the snapshot actually includes. Reconnect and retest account-specific integrations for every deployment. A copied configuration is not a substitute for acceptance testing.

Separate your reusable methods from the buyer's configured assets in the agreement. Make data export and transition arrangements clear. Whole sub-account transfers have eligibility and dependency rules; do not promise that every integration moves unchanged. [HighLevel transfer guide](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002031-sub-account-transfers-all-you-need-to-know).

## Build the next purchase into the conversation

At discovery, ask what happens after the first build. If another client is due next month, record the likely date. If the need is maintenance, identify which workflows will require scheduled checks. If the build is genuinely one-off, do not invent a recurring dependency.

Use this sequence:

1. At kickoff, agree the buyer's success criteria and next likely business event.
2. At handover, show what passed and what remains dependent on staff or providers.
3. Offer the eight-hour support plan only if there is a real queue or continuing operational need.
4. Seven to fourteen days after launch, review behaviour and any scoped defects.
5. Before the next onboarding date, ask whether the same process should be deployed for the next client.
6. After a successful second purchase or useful review, ask for a testimonial, case-study permission or one relevant introduction.

The support fee is for scheduled checks and reserved implementation capacity. Fourteen-day correction of your original scoped defects remains separate and does not consume the paid allowance.

Where capacity and recurring work justify it, the overseas sixteen-hour plan is US$1,200/month across up to six named sub-accounts. Its checks, calls and changes all consume the allowance; new launches remain separate. Use the same request queue and approval boundaries, warn at twelve hours, and stop at sixteen without approval. Use the buyer's actual allowance in updates and renewal discussions.

## The weekly update

Send a concise update on the same agreed day. Include a small amount of evidence and a clear next action.

> **Completed:** [Work and link to test or change record].
>
> **Observed:** [Factual workflow result or account issue].
>
> **Waiting on:** [Decision, owner and requested date].
>
> **Allowance:** [Hours used] of eight hours; [hours remaining].
>
> **Next:** [One priority for the coming week].

Avoid reporting fictitious value. “Three hours saved” needs a credible before-and-after estimate. “Twelve appointments booked” is an observed count; it does not prove twelve additional appointments caused by your work.

For a buyer on the larger plan, replace eight with sixteen in the allowance line. Do not promise an additional automation each month irrespective of the hours it requires.

## Monthly review and renewal

Review completed requests, recurring faults, delivery hours, handoff quality and the buyer's next client launches. For a direct business client, also discuss lead response, booking rate, attendance and follow-up completion using consistent definitions.

Before renewal, ask:

- Is there enough useful work for the reserved capacity next month?
- What would make the service more valuable?
- Are decisions or access slowing delivery?
- Should the next month use support, a fixed project or a pause?

Use this renewal pitch:

> The first build is complete, and we have [factual result]. You mentioned [next account or continuing work]. I can reserve up to eight delivery hours next month for checks and small changes across the three agreed accounts, at US$600. A new client launch would remain a separate sprint. Would that match the work you expect?

Do not make renewal depend on withholding credentials, configuration or documentation. The reason to return should be confidence in your service and continuing useful work.

## Referral request

> Glad the delivery worked well for your team. Do you know one agency owner dealing with a similar HighLevel onboarding backlog? An introduction would be useful. I can send a two-sentence description for you to forward.

For white-label work, obtain permission before naming the agency or its end client publicly. A private testimonial or anonymised process example may be more suitable.

## Support boundaries and incidents

Maintain one shared request queue and one communication channel. Define the service window and two hours of business-day overlap with the buyer. Acknowledge ordinary requests within one business day. For a serious sending error or duplicated customer communication, prioritise containment during your service window, tell the buyer what is known, and agree the next step.

Do not promise continuous monitoring, round-the-clock response or a fixed resolution time while operating alone. Keep a pause procedure, change record and restore plan for each production change. If work cannot fit the allowance, obtain approval for additional time or a separate project before continuing.
