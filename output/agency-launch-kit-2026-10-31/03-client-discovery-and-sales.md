# HighLevel Client Discovery and Sales

Win the first buyer through a relevant demonstration and a small paid project. Start with agency owners who already sell HighLevel services, then qualify whether they have a current delivery gap. The public presence of HighLevel is a reason to research an agency; it is not proof that it wants to outsource.

## Where to find prospects

| Channel | How to use it | What qualifies a prospect |
|---|---|---|
| Agency websites and Google search | Search UK marketing agencies that explicitly mention HighLevel, client CRM, lead nurturing or onboarding | Paying client services, an identifiable decision-maker and a plausible fit for the sprint |
| LinkedIn | Research owners, founders and delivery leads at those agencies; use the website to confirm the service | A recent client launch, hiring post, platform discussion or other relevant business signal |
| Upwork | Search GoHighLevel workflow, HighLevel automation, CRM onboarding and agency implementation jobs | Clear scope, a plausible budget, relevant client history and a fit for your actual skills |
| HighLevel communities | Answer specific questions with useful explanations and a short relevant demonstration | A person explicitly asking for implementation help, or permission to discuss work |
| Certified Admin directory | Consider certification and a profile after the offer is working | A credibility and possible inbound channel, rather than a guarantee of leads |
| Indian agency introductions | Meet advertising and web agencies in Lucknow; ask how they deliver client CRM and follow-up work | Existing client work with a specific implementation gap |

HighLevel describes directory placement as part of its Certified Admin program. Use the directory as an optional channel; do not postpone prospect conversations to collect credentials. [Official program details](https://www.gohighlevel.com/post/highlevel-admin-certified).

Useful search strings:

- `"GoHighLevel" "marketing agency" "UK"`
- `"HighLevel" "client onboarding" agency`
- `"GoHighLevel" "automotive marketing"`
- `"GoHighLevel" "home services" agency`
- `"HighLevel" "hiring" "agency"`
- `"marketing agency" "Lucknow" "CRM"`

Research one country at a time. Start with the UK; Australia is a later test if it fits your working hours and the same buyer problem. Do not purchase a large unqualified contact list before you can recognise a good prospect manually.

## Build a list of 60 qualified agencies

Record the business, country, website, relevant service, named decision-maker, public business contact route, observed signal, message date, reply, discovery notes and next action date. Store only what is needed for business prospecting.

Score each agency from zero to two on five factors:

1. Clear evidence of HighLevel use.
2. Existing client services and a repeatable onboarding process.
3. A current signal of workload, hiring, launch or implementation need.
4. Access to the owner or delivery decision-maker through an appropriate channel.
5. Fit for one client journey and the proposed paid pilot.

Prioritise those scoring seven or more out of ten. A score is a research aid; the discovery conversation determines whether an opportunity is real.

## What your proof should show

Create a fictional home-service client and synthetic contacts. Record a three-minute video showing:

1. A form submission creating the correct opportunity and owner notification.
2. Follow-up messages scheduled with the agreed delays.
3. A reply or booking stopping the appropriate follow-up.
4. A cancellation or reschedule changing reminders correctly.
5. A clear handover view explaining what the agency will maintain.

Label this as a demonstration. Never turn test contacts into supposed clients or imply measured sales improvements. The useful proof is that you can reason through the process, handle exceptions and deliver a maintainable setup.

Have one process diagram, a sample test record and a short explanation of how you diagnose a failed workflow. Those are more relevant to the first buyer than a long list of tools.

## Initial outreach

Use a public business email or an appropriate professional message. Be truthful about who you are, why the message is relevant and how to decline further contact. Check the applicable channel and destination rules before scaling outbound outreach.

**Email subject:** HighLevel onboarding capacity for [agency]

> Hi [name],
>
> I noticed [specific, accurate observation about the agency's service].
>
> I build and test HighLevel lead capture, follow-up and booking workflows for agencies. I work inside your account and provide the test record and handover, so your team can maintain the result.
>
> Are new client setups handled entirely in-house, or do you sometimes use an implementation partner?
>
> I have a three-minute demo of a one-client setup if that would be useful.
>
> [Your real name]
> Automation specialist based in Lucknow, India

Keep the observation specific. Do not assert that their automation is broken or they are losing money unless they have told you so. Do not pretend to have submitted a real enquiry or audited private systems.

**After a positive reply:**

> Here is the demo: [link]. It shows capture, follow-up stop rules and booking reminders, using test data. For a first engagement I offer one fixed-scope client setup at US$450. Is there a current account or onboarding project we could discuss for 15 minutes?

**Professional message version:**

> Hi [name], I saw your HighLevel service for [buyer type]. I handle workflow implementation and testing for agencies. Do you use outside delivery help when client onboardings overlap?

**Matched marketplace application:**

> Your requirement for [specific workflow] matches the process in this demo: [link]. I would first confirm [key dependency], then build [relevant steps], test [important exception] and hand over [documentation]. For the scope in your brief, my proposed milestone is [specific outcome and fee]. One question before I confirm: [genuine question that affects scope]?

Follow each marketplace's communication and payment rules. Do not copy a US$450 proposal into a job requiring a much larger migration.

## Follow up with useful context

For unanswered business outreach, use a restrained sequence and stop immediately on a decline or opt-out. Avoid contacting the same person across several channels at once.

| Timing | Message purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Three business days after first message | Clarify relevance | “The part I handle is the client workflow build and testing. Does that work sit with you or someone else?” |
| Seven business days after first message | Add a useful example | “One test in the demo checks that reminders change after rescheduling. Happy to send the checklist if your team would find it useful.” |
| Fourteen business days after first message | Close the sequence | “I will leave this here. If a HighLevel onboarding project needs extra delivery capacity later, I can scope one client build.” |

Do not use cold WhatsApp campaigns as the starting prospecting engine. WhatsApp's business policy requires the recipient's number or username and opt-in permission, and the Platform has template and service-window rules. For client journeys, record consent and honour opt-outs. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy).

## The discovery call

Keep the first call to about 20 minutes. Spend most of it on the buyer's current work.

1. What kind of clients do you onboard into HighLevel?
2. How many client setups are active or expected over the next month?
3. Who builds them today, and where does work wait?
4. Which specific account or workflow needs attention now?
5. What does the delay cost in owner time, rework or postponed launch?
6. Which source, calendar, messages and integrations are involved?
7. What is already functioning, and what still needs approval?
8. Who can grant access and approve the build?
9. What would make this first project successful?
10. If the pilot works, is the likely next purchase another setup, support or neither?

An agency with no current work, no budget and no approval owner is a nurture contact. Do not turn it into a proposal merely because the conversation was friendly.

## The pitch and close

**Short spoken pitch:**

> I help agencies deliver their clients' HighLevel lead and booking workflows. For one client, I configure the source, pipeline, calendar and three automations, test the stop rules and exceptions, then hand over the process and walkthrough. The first project is US$450 with a defined scope. If it fits your next onboarding, we can start with that account.

**At the end of discovery:**

> You said [confirmed bottleneck] is delaying [current project]. I can take the [specific journey] as the first sprint. The scope is [brief summary], the pilot fee is US$450, and delivery is seven business days after access and approvals. I will send a one-page scope with acceptance tests. Who needs to review it, and when can you decide?

Agree a decision date. Send the proposal the same day when the scope is clear. Follow up on the agreed date; do not repeatedly ask whether they have seen the document.

## Common objections

| Buyer says | Response |
|---|---|
| “We already have a person.” | “That may cover your needs. Do they also have capacity when several onboardings overlap? I can scope one overflow project if that becomes useful.” |
| “Why are you more than a cheap freelancer?” | “The fee includes a defined build, exception testing, documentation and handover. Compare the complete deliverable and responsibility. If you only need a small fix, I can scope that separately.” |
| “Can you guarantee bookings?” | “I can commit to the agreed workflow functioning and being tested. Actual bookings also depend on lead quality, the offer and the client's follow-up.” |
| “We want someone local.” | “I am based in Lucknow. We can agree an overlap window and clear written updates. If you need physical attendance or local-only staff, I may not fit.” |
| “Can you do a free trial?” | “I can show the demo and test record. Production work starts with the paid pilot and its acceptance criteria.” |
| “We have no project now.” | “When is your next likely onboarding? With your agreement I can check back then.” |

## Selling through Indian agencies

Ask for introductions to agencies already running Meta or Google campaigns for local businesses. Their issue to investigate is how enquiries are assigned, followed up and reported after the campaign generates them.

Use this pitch:

> Aap ads aur client relationship handle kijiye. Main enquiry capture, follow-up aur booking ka HighLevel setup build aur test karunga. Pehle ek client ka defined paid pilot karte hain. Agar delivery useful lage, agle clients ke liye repeat kar sakte hain.

For a direct business owner, ask about monthly enquiries and the last ten enquiries that did not convert. Sell one confirmed operational improvement. Do not lead with the full service catalogue.
